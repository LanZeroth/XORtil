HARMATTAN

Twenty-five years of fire. Two satellite eras. One calendar.

Every dry season, the Harmattan wind sweeps south and Nigeria burns: savanna, farmland and forest edge. Satellites have watched since 2000, but MODIS and VIIRS see fire differently, so their records cannot be compared directly. Harmattan merges them into one consistent record and turns it into a calendar of when and where burning happens.

Each turn of the spiral is one year, starting in the centre in late 2000 and moving outward to today. January is at the top. Brighter means more burning. The shaded wedge marks the critical period, the part of the year that historically holds most of the burning.

Burning activity calendar

One row per year and one column per day. Nigeria's fire season crosses New Year, so the "fire year" view (July to June) keeps each season in one row. Click a cell to jump the map to that month.

What is normal, and when is it critical?

The band shows the 10th–90th percentile of daily burning (15-day smoothed) over the 2003–2022 baseline, and the line shows the median. Use it to see whether a season starts early, peaks high, or ends late.

Month-by-month anomaly

Anomaly = standard deviations from the 2003–2022 baseline for the same month. Red means more burning than usual, blue means less.

Why the records needed harmonizing

VIIRS (375 m) sees many small fires that MODIS (1 km) misses. Placed side by side, the switch from MODIS to VIIRS would look like a jump in burning that never happened. Harmattan converts both records to a common unit, active-fire cell-days on a 0.1° grid, then calibrates VIIRS to the MODIS scale for each 1° tile. The calibration is fitted on 2012–2021 and tested on 2022–2025.
